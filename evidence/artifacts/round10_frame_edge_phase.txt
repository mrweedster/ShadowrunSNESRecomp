   `generic_frame_driver.c` mentions `master_cycles` on two lines only

   says, and it is why the phase cannot be an artefact of how the tap

  Tier of each anchor, control/seed: `$00C0E2` is the NMI handler and is
  interpreted on both legs.  `$80C530` is interpreted on the control (it
  is not in the shipped pair) and compiled AOT on the seed (it is in the
  demoted set); that is the entire difference between the two legs.
  Caveat on the entry counts: the AOT hook can only fire where the
  compiled function's own entry pc is the anchor, while the interp hook
  fires at every opcode start at that pc.  A pass that enters the seed's
  root mid-body is invisible to the AOT hook, so the two counts are
  tap-site counts, not call counts; the offset columns compare the first
  entry each leg can report, which both tiers reach as an entry.

== 7. what this decides ==

  The frame-edge offset that round 8 measured at f1832 (+14) is one
  sample of a bounded, self-cancelling quantity, not a charge that
  accumulates:
  * over the 4574 shared boundaries it is 0 on 4095 and never leaves -58..+70;
  * it appears in 241 separate runs, 152 of them one frame long, longest 16 frames;
  * both legs cover exactly 1634601236 master clocks across the route, so nothing is lost;
  * the guest cycle counter `cpu->cycles` by contrast ends at -1309 and reaches -2609, which is the counter
    `evidence/upstream_seam_cycle_bookkeeping.md` says cannot be compared across tiers.

  Where it comes from, in the pinned `generic_frame_driver.c`: the frame
  deadline is `frame_end = master_cycles + 357368`, taken from wherever
  the previous frame ended, the NMI is taken once before the slice loop,
  a pending IRQ is serviced after each slice, and the loop tests
  `master_cycles >= frame_end` only at the top of a slice.  So the
  achieved boundary is the first instruction boundary at or after the
  deadline that the loop can notice, and the overshoot is a whole number
  of block or interrupt-take lengths.  Changing which bodies are compiled
  changes which block that test first becomes true inside, which moves
  the boundary by one such quantum - and at f1832 one quantum was enough
  to move a 66-clock poll across a hardware edge both legs agree on.

  That leaves exactly four conceivable levers, and all four are closed:
  1. make the seed's block boundaries coincide with the control's at every
     frame - impossible for a different body set; the offset is non-zero
     on 479 boundaries route-wide, and it starts at f1767, 63 frames before the first
     differing state byte;
  2. make the guest's use of the edge insensitive to entry phase - the
     read-time change of round 7 tried the only version of this available
     to the title and made it worse (305 -> 1,333 differing frames);
  3. price the seed's bodies so the overshoot comes out identical - that
     is tuning the cycle model to a one-frame coincidence, and the
     `+-40` charge at call boundaries that round 8 section 9 left unnamed
     is still unnamed; round 9 watched it appear and cancel, so it is
     not a lever either;
  4. make the frame driver's deadline check land at instruction
     granularity, so the overshoot stops being quantized - that is a
     change to `runner/src/generic_frame_driver.c`, which is the pinned
     framework, and the pin stays clean.

  Decision: no seed is adopted, no `force_lle` block is added, and this
  line of investigation is closed.  The shipped pair stays
  `$80C399` + `$80AAB3`, executable a0587ed0a0e34687.  Anything further
  here needs an upstream seam change, not a seed.
